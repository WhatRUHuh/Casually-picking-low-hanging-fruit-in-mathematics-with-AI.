# Fruit 005 — Asymptotic growth of sums of square roots

**Status:** candidate proof; exact finite checks passed; independent review, novelty check and Lean formalization pending.

**Displayed thinking time:** 55 seconds (reported by the human initiator; not total proof or verification time).

Source: [OEIS A397045](https://oeis.org/A397045). Sequence by Ali Sada, June 14, 2026; asymptotic conjecture and suggested constant by Charles R. Greathouse IV, June 15, 2026.

## Theorem

Let a(n), n>=1, be the number of distinct values in [n,n+1) representable as finite sums of square roots of positive integers. Then

$$\log a(n)\sim K n^{2/3},\qquad K=\left(\frac{81\zeta(3)}{\pi^2}\right)^{1/3}=2.144717522977\ldots.$$

This establishes the proposed logarithmic growth rate and constant, subject to independent checking of the argument below. It does not assert a multiplicative asymptotic for a(n) itself.

## 1. Reduction to weighted partitions

Let D be the squarefree integers greater than 1. Every representable value can be written as

$$c_1+\sum_{d\in D}c_d\sqrt d,$$

where all coefficients are nonnegative integers of finite support. Distinct squarefree radicals (including 1) are linearly independent over the rationals. For a finite collection, take the multiquadratic field generated by square roots of all primes occurring in it. The prime square classes are independent in Q*/Q*², so its sign-change automorphisms form the full elementary abelian 2-group; averaging against the distinct sign characters isolates each radical coefficient. Thus this representation is unique.

Write S(c)=sum_{d in D} c_d sqrt(d). For each c with S(c)<n+1 there is exactly one nonnegative integer c_1 satisfying n<=c_1+S(c)<n+1, namely ceil(n-S(c)). Conversely any representable number in this interval has such a c. The empty vector gives the integer n. Hence a(n)=N(n+1), where N(X) counts finitely supported nonnegative integer vectors with S(c)<X. This count is finite because only d<X² can occur, and each coefficient is bounded by X/sqrt(d).

## 2. Laplace sums and partition function

Define B(x)=#{d in D : sqrt(d)<=x}. The classical squarefree estimate gives

$$B(x)=\frac6{\pi^2}x^2+O(x)\quad(x\to\infty).$$

Indeed the squarefree indicator is sum_{q²|d} mu(q), where mu is the Möbius function. Summing up to x², the floor errors sum to O(x); extending the absolutely convergent sum of mu(q)/q² contributes O(x). Its Euler product is 1/zeta(2)=6/pi². Excluding 1 only changes the count by at most one.

For j=0,1,2 define

$$H_j(u)=\sum_{d\in D}d^{j/2}e^{-u\sqrt d}.$$

By Stieltjes integration by parts, or convergence of the rescaled counting measures,

$$H_j(u)\sim \frac{12(j+1)!}{\pi^2 u^{j+2}}\quad(u\downarrow0).$$

For detail, integrate x^j e^(-ux) against dB(x). The leading term from B(x)=6x²/pi² is (12/pi²) times the integral of x^(j+1)e^(-ux), giving the displayed constant. The error O(x) in B contributes O(u^(-j-1)), by integrating the absolute value of the derivative of x^j e^(-ux) against O(x). Boundary terms vanish. The bound B(x)<=x² for all x>=0 gives H_j(u)<=C_j u^(-j-2) for every u>0 by the same integration by parts estimate.

For t>0 set

$$Z(t)=\sum_c e^{-tS(c)}=\prod_{d\in D}(1-e^{-t\sqrt d})^{-1}.$$

The product converges since H_0(t)<infinity; the equality follows by monotone convergence over finite subsets of D. Expanding the logarithm,

$$\log Z(t)=\sum_{m\ge1}\frac{H_0(mt)}m.$$

The uniform H_0 bound and the convergence of sum m^(-3) justify dominated convergence after multiplying by t². Therefore, with A=12 zeta(3)/pi²,

$$\log Z(t)\sim A t^{-2}.$$

## 3. Upper bound

For every t>0, N(X)<=e^(tX)Z(t). Choosing t=(2A/X)^(1/3) gives

$$\limsup_{X\to\infty}X^{-2/3}\log N(X)\le3(A/4)^{1/3}.$$

## 4. Matching lower bound

On the countable set of finite-support vectors put P_t(c)=e^(-tS(c))/Z(t). Equivalently the coordinates are independent geometric random variables with parameters q_d=e^(-t sqrt(d)), assigning probability (1-q_d)q_d^k to k>=0. The product distribution is supported on finite-support vectors almost surely by the summability of q_d (Borel–Cantelli).

Using the geometric means and variances, and expanding q/(1-q) and q/(1-q)^2, we obtain

$$\mathbb E_t S=\sum_{m\ge1}H_1(mt)\sim 2At^{-3},$$

$$\operatorname{Var}_t S=\sum_{m\ge1}mH_2(mt)\sim6At^{-4}.$$

Both limits follow by dominated convergence with the uniform bounds above; in each case the summable dominating series is proportional to sum m^(-3). In particular the sums are finite for each t>0. Independence justifies adding variances, using convergence in L² of the finite partial sums (the means and variances are summable).

Fix 0<delta<1 and put t=(2A/((1-delta/2)X))^(1/3). Then E_t S / X tends to 1-delta/2, while Var_t S / X²=O(X^(-2/3)). Chebyshev's inequality implies

$$P_t((1-\delta)X\le S<X)\longrightarrow1.$$

On this event each vector has probability at most e^(-t(1-delta)X)/Z(t). Consequently

$$N(X)\ge Z(t)e^{t(1-\delta)X}P_t((1-\delta)X\le S<X).$$

Writing beta=1-delta/2, taking logarithms and dividing by X^(2/3) gives a lower limit at least

$$A(\beta/(2A))^{2/3}+(1-\delta)(2A/\beta)^{1/3}.$$

Now let delta decrease to zero. This yields the matching lower bound 3(A/4)^(1/3).

## 5. Conclusion

Combining the bounds yields log N(X) ~ 3(A/4)^(1/3) X^(2/3). Since a(n)=N(n+1),

$$K=3(A/4)^{1/3}=\left(\frac{81\zeta(3)}{\pi^2}\right)^{1/3}.$$

QED (candidate argument, pending independent review).
